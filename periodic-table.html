🔴 Alkali Metals — Group 1 (except H)
3LiLithium 11NaSodium 19KPotassium 37RbRubidium 55CsCesium 87FrFrancium

Soft, shiny metals that react vigorously with water — producing hydrogen gas and heat. Their reactivity increases down the group. Cesium is so reactive it explodes on contact with water, even ice! 💥

🟠 Alkaline Earth Metals — Group 2
4BeBeryllium 12MgMagnesium 20CaCalcium 38SrStrontium 56BaBarium 88RaRadium

Harder and less reactive than alkali metals, but still quite reactive. They form strongly basic (alkaline) oxides. Calcium is essential for bones; magnesium powers thousands of enzymes in your body. Strontium produces brilliant red fireworks! 🎆

🔵 Transition Metals — Groups 3–12

The largest block of elements! Typically hard, shiny, and excellent conductors of heat and electricity. Many form brightly colored compounds and are vital in industry, biology, and technology. 🏗️

🟢 Post-Transition Metals
13AlAluminium 31GaGallium 49InIndium 50SnTin 81TlThallium 82PbLead 83BiBismuth 113NhNihonium 114FlFlerovium 115McMoscovium 116LvLivermorium

Softer and have lower melting points than transition metals. Gallium melts in your hand at just 29.8°C! Tin was crucial to the Bronze Age when alloyed with copper. Lead's toxicity made ancient Rome's lead pipes a public health disaster. 🏺

🟣 Metalloids — The Middle Ground
5BBoron 14SiSilicon 32GeGermanium 33AsArsenic 51SbAntimony 52TeTellurium 84PoPolonium

The chameleons of the periodic table — they have properties between metals and nonmetals! Semiconductors like silicon and germanium are the engines of modern electronics. Arsenic was history's favorite poison and is now used in cancer treatments. ⚡

🌿 Reactive Nonmetals
1HHydrogen 6CCarbon 7NNitrogen 8OOxygen 15PPhosphorus 16SSulfur 34SeSelenium

Despite being only a handful of elements, nonmetals make up the vast majority of living matter. Carbon alone forms more compounds than all other elements combined! Oxygen and nitrogen together make up 99% of the air you breathe. 🌱

💛 Halogens — Group 17
9FFluorine 17ClChlorine 35BrBromine 53IIodine 85AtAstatine 117TsTennessine

From a gas (F, Cl) to a liquid (Br) to a solid (I) — the only group to exist in all three states at room temperature! Halogens ("salt-formers") all react readily with metals to form salts. Bromine is the only nonmetal that is liquid at room temperature. 🧪

💎 Noble Gases — Group 18
2HeHelium 10NeNeon 18ArArgon 36KrKrypton 54XeXenon 86RnRadon 118OgOganesson

Odorless, colorless gases with completely full outer electron shells — making them almost totally unreactive. Each one glows a unique color in a discharge tube: neon red, argon violet, krypton white, xenon blue. They're used in lasers, lighting, and MRI contrast agents. 🌈

✨ Lanthanides — the "Rare Earths"
57LaLanthanum 58CeCerium 59PrPraseodymium 60NdNeodymium 61PmPromethium 62SmSamarium 63EuEuropium
64GdGadolinium 65TbTerbium 66DyDysprosium 67HoHolmium 68ErErbium 69TmThulium 70YbYtterbium
71LuLutetium

Despite being called "rare earths," most lanthanides are actually fairly abundant in Earth's crust. They have extraordinary magnetic, optical, and electronic properties that power modern technology — from smartphone screens (Eu, Tb) and wind turbine magnets (Nd, Dy) to fiber optic amplifiers (Er). 🌿

All actinides are radioactive! Elements beyond Uranium (92) are "transuranic" — synthesized artificially in nuclear reactors or particle accelerators. Americium-241 is in most smoke detectors. Curium was named after Marie and Pierre Curie. Plutonium powered the Voyager probes! ⚛️
